The "Landing" Era (2011-2022)

A few years after setting up initial plans for Mew151.net, I tore it all down out of shame. I wanted to keep the domain and hosting, but I didn't want to see that Mew banner or the naive promises to singlehandedly build a Neopets knock-off. And so I replaced it all with an empty landing page. White monospace text. That's it.

Initially it remained empty. Just a single page that didn't link anywhere and turned away any curious visitors. But if I really didn't care about having a website anymore, why did I continue to pay for a domain and web hosting? Clearly in my heart I still wanted to have a website but... for some reason I never built anything to replace that static landing page.

In 2014, I finally added a subtle, nearly invisible link to the main Mew151.net landing page. It was a link to my then-new art project { bios }, something I wanted to share publicly. It was a good match for the empty and impersonal landing page.

In the next few years, I got the idea to start some kind of blog about anime. The original concept was an anime-themed equivalent to Clickhole that my sister and I titled Musume Disorder. The blog would have various characters who posted strange humorous content about anime that parodied other anime fan websites. This website never got developed quite far, but what was built out became something else.

In 2017, I started my blog, Perfect Pop Star Academy. Running on a fork of the Musume Disorder blogging engine called Popstar Disorder, I originally planned to post episode-by-episode reviews of Aikatsu. I'd then hopefully expand to other idol anime series, and slowly build up a collection of funny posts that drove home how similar but delightful the tropes of idol anime were. While I wrote some initial test posts in this style, I never publicly posted them, and instead focused on just getting any content on my blog at all. By 2017, the blog launched and I linked to it on the Mew151.net landing page in a similar way to {bios}.

Near the end of 2018 I developed the website for the Home Age Conversations podcast. Although it was pretty much a completely different project, it actually inherited some pieces of code originally written for Musume Disorder. I linked to the podcast website on my landing page as well once it was up.

Finally in 2021, I decided to post about games I made mostly during my childhood. I created my Gamez Archive with Zonelets and linked to it on the landing page. At this point, all four corners of the page linked to different things. It's clear that even during this "empty" era of my website, there was still growth and movement, and these "sub-sites" as I refer to them now were little sproutlings trying to grow up to be something more. But despite it all, the main "thing" at Mew151.net was still an empty page with nothing else going on.

The "landing" era of the site. There are links in all four corners of the screen.

In 2022 I realized I wanted to actually have a website and not just a boring landing page. I rediscovered the personal web and found it alive and thriving on places like Neocities. And so I started crafting the next vision of my website. Something that finally deserved to live at Mew151.net.

The day before the new site launched, I made one final update to the landing page, and submitted it to be archived by the Wayback Machine.
